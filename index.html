01

Biography

Tim Berners-Lee

Sir Tim Berners-Lee is a British computer scientist best known as the inventor of the World Wide Web.

He was born on 8 June 1955 in London, England. His parents were mathematicians and computer scientists who worked on one of the earliest commercial computers.

Berners-Lee studied physics at Queen's College, Oxford. After graduating, he worked on several computer and technology projects before joining CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research.

While working at CERN, he noticed that scientists needed an easier way to share information between different computers. This idea eventually led him to develop the foundations of the World Wide Web.

02

The Birth of the Web

1989 The Proposal

In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee proposed a system that would allow information to be connected and accessed across computers.

He developed important technologies including HTML, HTTP and URLs. Together, these technologies became fundamental parts of the World Wide Web.

1990 The First Website

Berners-Lee created the first web server and web browser. He also created the first website, which explained how the World Wide Web worked.

1991 The Web Goes Public

The World Wide Web became available to people outside CERN, helping the technology spread around the world.

04

His Legacy

"The web is more a social creation than a technical one."

Tim Berners-Lee has continued to advocate for an open and accessible web. He founded the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), an organization that develops web standards.

His invention transformed communication, education, business, entertainment and access to information. Today, billions of people use the web for everyday activities.

Did you know?

Berners-Lee chose not to patent the World Wide Web, allowing the technology to develop openly.
